# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management process documents used by OctoAcme. Together, these guides provide a consistent framework for moving work from idea to delivery while keeping teams aligned on priorities, ownership, and execution.

## Overview

OctoAcme uses a lightweight, cross-functional project management model that begins with initiation and ends with retrospective. Teams validate the business need, confirm stakeholders, and document the problem, goals, success metrics, and initial risks before work moves into planning. This front-end structure ensures the project has a clear purpose and measurable outcome before the team commits delivery effort.

Once the initiative is approved, work moves into planning, where backlog items are prioritized, dependencies are mapped, milestones are defined, and responsibilities are assigned. The core roles are clearly separated but collaborative: Product leads define outcomes and prioritization, Project Managers coordinate schedules, risks, and communication, developers build and validate the solution, QA verifies quality and acceptance, and stakeholders provide input and approval. This creates shared accountability and reduces gaps between strategy, execution, and delivery.

During execution, the team relies on recurring delivery rhythms such as standups, weekly syncs, demos, and project tracking to maintain momentum and visibility. Communication is treated as an operational requirement, with status updates, risk registers, and escalation paths used to surface blockers early and keep stakeholders informed. Quality assurance is embedded throughout delivery through acceptance criteria, CI checks, security scanning, unit and integration tests, smoke tests, and manual QA where needed. Before release, the team verifies deployment readiness, smoke tests, rollback planning, and post-deploy checks to reduce production risk.

At the close of each milestone or release, OctoAcme emphasizes continuous improvement through structured retrospectives, action tracking, and follow-up on previously identified issues. This cycle of initiation, planning, execution, release, and review creates a repeatable project management system that balances speed, accountability, quality, and learning across the full project lifecycle.

## Project management process summary

- Initiate work by confirming the business need, stakeholders, and success criteria.
- Plan the work by defining milestones, backlog priorities, dependencies, and ownership.
- Execute through structured delivery rhythms, including standups, reviews, and project tracking.
- Manage risk and communication with clear escalation paths and regular stakeholder updates.
- Release and validate changes with checkpoints, smoke tests, and deployment readiness checks.
- Close each cycle with retrospectives and action items to improve the process over time.
